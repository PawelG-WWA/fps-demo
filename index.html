40 FPS on Fixed 60 vs. 120 Hz

At a fixed 120 Hz, each 40 FPS frame occupies three refreshes (25.0 ms). At a fixed 60 Hz, the model alternates 2-refresh and 1-refresh holds—33.3 ms and 16.7 ms—producing uneven 2–1 cadence.

Interpolation Buffer

This interpolation example uses about 67 ms of buffering delay (two 30 FPS source intervals). Interpolation can smooth motion while delaying the state being shown; actual delay depends on implementation.

Slow-Mo Inspector

Quarter-speed playback stretches the simulated timing pattern so individual refresh steps are easier to inspect. It illustrates cadence rather than reproducing normal-speed perception.

Budget & Spikes

In this model, all targets use the same scene-cost assumption. A 30 ms total frame cost fits inside a 30 FPS budget (33.3 ms), but exceeds the 25.0 ms and 16.7 ms budgets for 40 and 60 FPS.

MODEL

How This Model Works

The lab compares target FPS rates using a simplified deterministic simulation, a selected workload, and a simulated fixed-refresh display. Real engines and monitors behave differently due to CPU/GPU overlap, render queues, variable refresh rate (VRR), and display processing.

1. Configured vs. Computed vs. Observed

Configured: Simulated refresh (60 / 120 Hz), Load (1.0–5.0), Performance Level (1–5), 30 Hz Simulation environment (1–5), and 0.25× playback speed. Computed: FPS, frame intervals, landing drift, and model latency. Observed: Only the top-right browser cadence indicator samples requestAnimationFrame callback deltas.

2. Workload, Spikes & 30 Hz Integrator

Motion, Pipeline, and Refresh Map model one combined frame cost (14.0 + (Load − 1) × 7.5 ms), plus transient spikes (+17.5 ms). Performance scales exponentially (12.0 × 1.52^(Level − 1)). Section 03 compares a fixed 1/30 s accumulator against naive variable-dt Euler + piecewise gravity integration and tick-locked stepping.

3. Fixed-Refresh V-Sync Quantization

Completed frames snap to discrete simulated refresh ticks (16.67 ms at 60 Hz, 8.33 ms at 120 Hz). V-Sync is assumed; VRR and screen tearing are not modeled. Quarter-speed (0.25×) stretches playback time only and does not alter reported statistics.

4. Simulated Frame Interpolation & Latency

Simulated frame interpolation runs a 30 FPS source and holds a chosen 2× source-interval buffer (about 67 ms at baseline), outputting at the simulated refresh rate. Artifacts are illustrative. Motion reports mid-screen latency (½ scanout), while Pipeline reports bottom-of-screen latency (full scanout + 2.5 ms assumed gray-to-gray pixel response).
